In this folder there is a MaxQuant proteinGroups.txt from a published experiment and a
sample_metadata.csv describing the samples. Read both before you plan anything. Do not
print whole files - they are very large. Look at the column names and the first rows.

Goal: find the proteins that differ between the two conditions, and make a volcano plot
I would be willing to put in a figure.

Keep a file called NOTES.md and update it after every step with: the goal, the decisions
made and why, constraints to remember, the current state, what did not work, and the next
step.

Work through the steps below. After step 1, stop and wait until I type "continue". Then do
the remaining steps without stopping, and show me the result of each step as you go.

1. ORIENT
   How many protein groups and samples are there? Which columns hold the quantities? How do
   the samples map to conditions? Tell me if anything in the metadata does not match the
   data.

2. FILTER
   Remove reverse hits, potential contaminants, and proteins only identified by site. Say
   how many rows each filter removed. MaxQuant writes missing values as 0: treat them as
   missing, not as zero abundance, and say how many there are.

3. CHECK THE ASSUMPTIONS - DO NOT ASSUME THEM
   Test whether the intensities are normally distributed: Shapiro-Wilk per protein, plus a
   histogram and a QQ plot for a few proteins. If they are not normal, log2-transform them
   and test again. Show the evidence before and after, not just a statement.
   Decide whether the samples need normalising against each other, say why, and show the
   sample distributions before and after.

4. DECIDE THE TEST
   There are two conditions, so use a t-test. Check whether the variances are similar
   (Levene) and choose Student's or Welch's accordingly.
   Look carefully at the metadata: is anything making the samples not independent? If so,
   tell me what it is and what it means for the analysis.
   Choose a minimum number of valid values per group, and say why.

5. TEST AND CORRECT
   Run the test and correct for multiple testing with Benjamini-Hochberg. Report how many
   proteins were tested, how many pass FDR < 0.05, and how many also pass |log2FC| > 1.

6. VOLCANO PLOT
   Plot log2 fold change against -log10 p. Colour significant proteins by direction and the
   rest grey. Label the most extreme genes. Write the direction of the comparison on the
   axis so nobody can misread the sign. Save it as volcano.png.

7. CHECK YOURSELF
   - Print the top 15 up and top 15 down, with gene name, log2FC, p and q.
   - Compare the direction of the top hits with what is known about this condition. Tell me
     honestly whether it looks plausible.
   - Shuffle the condition labels, re-run steps 3 to 5, and report how many proteins pass
     FDR in the shuffled data. If that is not close to zero, something is wrong - tell me.
   - Restate n at every stage: loaded -> filtered -> tested -> significant.

Finally, save one script, analysis.py, that reproduces everything from the raw file; the
full results table as results.csv, one row per tested protein, with the columns gene,
log2FC, p and q (the knowledge-base exercise uses it); and a short RESULTS.md saying what you did, what you
chose and why, and what you are unsure about. Use a fixed random seed.
